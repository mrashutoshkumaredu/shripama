Doctoral thesis

Resilience & Digital Transformation of Women Vendors in Conflict-Prone Manipur

A mixed-methods study of Ima Keithel, Imphal — examining how conflict-related disruption shapes the livelihoods of women vendors, and the role digital technology plays in helping them cope, adapt, and transform.

Digital Divide Theory Resilience Theory Mixed Methods 210 Vendors Surveyed
How the thesis evolved

From "Ema Market" to a full resilience framework

My research question has sharpened across four Doctoral Research Committee (DRC) reviews — from an early exploration of digital technology in Ema Keithel to a focused study of resilience and digital transformation.

2nd DRC · Jun – Dec 2024

Exploring the Role of Digital Technologies in a Conflict-Prone Socio-Political Environment: A Study of Ema Market in Manipur, India

Framed the initial research problem: how digital technologies could help women vendors bridge gaps created by political unrest, ethnic tension, and economic instability.

3rd DRC · Jan – May 2025

Exploring the Role of Digital Technologies in Conflict-Prone Area: A Study of Ima Market, Manipur

Refined the title and conducted a need assessment with vendors across product categories — surfacing real barriers to digital literacy, trust, and confidence.

4th & 5th DRC · 2025 – 2026

Resilience and Digital Transformation of Women Vendors in Conflict-Prone, Manipur

Settled on the current title, grounded the study in Digital Divide Theory and Resilience Theory, formalised an Adopt–Adapt–Transform conceptual framework, and moved into full-scale quantitative fieldwork (210 vendors, Jan–Mar 2026).

Research gap

Three gaps this study addresses

Knowledge gap

Existing studies examine informal economies, conflict-affected livelihoods, resilience, and digital transformation separately — rarely how they interact for women vendors specifically.

Population gap

Most research focuses on urban, formal-sector entrepreneurs — overlooking women working daily in conflict-affected informal markets like Ima Keithel.

Methodological gap

Prior work leans on purely qualitative or purely quantitative designs. This study uses an explanatory sequential mixed-methods design to capture both scale and depth.

Research questions

What I'm asking

  • How do conflict-related disruptions affect the livelihood activities of women vendors of Ima Keithel?
  • What adaptive strategies do women vendors employ to cope with conflict-related disruptions?
  • How do digital technology adoption and adaptive livelihood strategies contribute to livelihood transformation and improved outcomes?
  • What challenges do women vendors face in accessing digital technologies, and how do socio-demographic factors influence that relationship?
Objectives

Four objectives guiding the thesis

01 · Impact

Examine how conflict-related disruptions affect the livelihood activities of women vendors.

02 · Adaptation

Identify the adaptive livelihood strategies vendors use in response to those disruptions.

03 · Transformation

Study the relationship between digital technology adoption, adaptive strategies, and livelihood outcomes.

04 · Barriers

Examine the challenges women vendors face accessing digital technologies in a conflict-prone area.

Conceptual framework

Adopt → Adapt → Transform

Conflict and disruption trigger a coping mechanism — vendors adopt digital tools, adapt their livelihood strategies, and over time transform their businesses — shaped throughout by facilitating conditions, effort expectancy, and performance expectancy.

Theoretical framework

Two lenses

Digital Divide Theory

Explores disparities in access to technology across first-level (access), second-level (skills & usage), and third-level (impact on outcomes) divides. (Van Dijk, 2005)

Resilience Theory

Explains how individuals, businesses, and communities adapt to and recover from adversity, and the strategies that sustain livelihoods amid instability. (Burnside, 2022)

Hypotheses

What I'm testing

H1

Conflict-related disruptions significantly influence the adoption of adaptive livelihood strategies among women vendors.

H2

Digital technology adoption positively influences the livelihood outcomes of women vendors in conflict-prone areas.

H3

Socio-demographic factors (age, education, product type, digital awareness) significantly influence digital technology adoption.

Methodology

Explanatory sequential mixed methods

Quantitative data is collected and analysed first, then qualitative fieldwork explains and contextualises the findings — allowing a fuller picture of vendors' digital engagement, barriers, and resilience strategies.

Design

Explanatory sequential mixed methods

Emerging themes

What the field is telling me

Digital literacy & use

Vendors show widely varied digital engagement — from confident use of video calls and mobile payments to basic keypad phones used only for calls. Many are in a transitional stage.

Impact of conflict

Conflict brings fewer customers, business interruptions, and real economic strain — pushing some vendors toward loans or alternative livelihoods.

Barriers to digitalisation

Low trust, limited digital literacy, generational gaps, and infrastructure limits persist. Owning a QR scanner doesn't always mean digital confidence.

Research ethics

How I protect participants

Informed consent

All participants are informed of the study's purpose and their right to confidentiality.

Anonymity

Data is anonymised to protect the identity of every participant.

Cultural sensitivity

Guidelines ensure the research does not disrupt Ima Keithel's social fabric, especially given the political context.

Community engagement

Local organisations are involved to keep the study culturally appropriate and respectful of local norms.
