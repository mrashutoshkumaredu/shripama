About me

Researching technology at the edge of conflict and community

I am a PhD scholar in the Department of Society Technology Interface at the Central University of Rajasthan, working under the supervision of Dr. Wairokpam Premi Devi. My research sits at the intersection of digital technology, gender, and conflict studies — asking how women entrepreneurs adapt, adopt, and transform their livelihoods when the ground beneath them keeps shifting.

Before focusing on Ima Keithel, my early doctoral work also explored the digital spaces of women artisans practising Kashida embroidery in the Thar Desert of Western Rajasthan — a study that has since been accepted for publication in the Journal of Asian and African Studies.

Doctoral research

Resilience & Digital Transformation of Women Vendors in Conflict-Prone Manipur

A mixed-methods study of Ima Keithel — the historic, women-run market of Imphal — examining how conflict-related disruption shapes livelihood strategies, and how digital tools help vendors cope, adapt, and transform.

The setting

Ima Keithel ("Mother's Market") is run entirely by women — over 3,600 registered vendors across three market complexes — and has weathered decades of socio-political unrest in Manipur.

The framework

Grounded in Digital Divide Theory and Resilience Theory, the study traces a coping mechanism of Adopt → Adapt → Transform from conflict disruption to livelihood outcomes.

The method

An explanatory sequential mixed-methods design — stratified survey of 210 vendors, followed by key informant interviews, FGDs and case studies to explain the numbers.
